### 2. Critiques of the current layout the articles would raise

1. Hierarchy: crest-blue tile is the right dominant element, but three same-weight cards below plus three tiles gives roughly 7 competing blocks. Squint test likely shows now tile, then a gray mass. Differentiate card priority (Today > News > Classes) or shrink Classes.
2. Hero greeting: pushes the key answer down and carries no task. Collapse to a single line or merge greeting into the now tile.
3. Scanning pattern: layer-cake needs distinct card headings; F-pattern means the left column must hold the most valuable content (Today). Ensure Today is top-left on desktop and first after the strip on phone.
4. Zero counts: three tiles showing "0" give the same prominence to nothing as to something. Show zero state de-emphasized (muted but still contrast-compliant, "None due", checkmark) and give non-zero tiles the emphasis. Do not hide the tile (layout shifts, recognition lost); do change its weight and text. Do not use low-contrast gray to do this: shrink the numeral and keep text at 4.5:1.
5. Color for meaning: update badges and tile accents must not rely on hue alone; reserve red/orange for urgency; keep blue as brand/primary only, not also as "unread" and "update" semantics at once.
6. Dark mode: brand blue #1446a0 on navy is low contrast; no shadows means elevation must come from lightness steps; hairline dividers disappear on dark; photos (news lead) glare in dark mode (consider slight dim or rounded frame); fixed-color crest asset may vanish.
7. Progress bar: needs direct labels (start/end times, time left as text), not just a fill; ensure fill vs track contrast is at least 3:1 in both themes.
8. Cards for homogeneous lists: rows inside cards must be uniform; per-class menus and pin/mark-read add secondary controls to every row, which is clutter. Show on hover/focus on desktop but keep always reachable on touch (a visible icon or whole-row sheet).
9. Cognitive load: three data types in the glance strip (time, counts, updates) plus three lists exceeds working-memory chunking; "Class updates" overlaps with the update badges in Classes list (redundant signal). Pick one primary place and link between them.
10. Proximity on phone: stacked tiles read as one group; counts row of 3 in a row is good, but gap rules should separate the "now" tile from the counts.
11. Information scent: tile labels (Due soon, Unread messages, Class updates) are decent; add "what opens" cues (chevron/whole tile link) and name the soonest due item under the count if space allows.
12. Hit targets: popovers become bottom sheets (good). Check tile and row targets at least 44px on phone and that the class menu button is not tiny.
13. Legibility: small meta text (news dates, "then X") is the first place light mode wins and dark mode degrades; keep 12px+ and full contrast tier.

## Laws of UX distilled for the school portal home dashboard

Source: all 36 Laws of UX law pages plus the 8 essays (cognitive load, Occam, Familiar vs Novel, Onboarding for Active Users, Tesler, Peak-End, Psychology of Design, Google Search). Page text treated as data. Item counts and pixel values below are my inferences from the laws, not numbers the pages state, except where marked "(page)".

### 1. Relevant laws, ranked by impact

1. **Selective Attention + Von Restorff (isolation effect).** People filter to goal-relevant stimuli; the one element that differs gets remembered. Apply: the crest-blue "class on now" tile is the only filled brand-colour block, which is right. Keep it the sole one. Do not let count tiles, news lead photo, or badges compete in strong colour. Change blindness: if timetable, counts and news all refresh at once, nothing is noticed; update the live tile quietly and only flag count changes. Never rely on colour alone for "due soon" or "unread" (add a number or icon). Keep news away from anything ad-like (banner blindness): no wide photo banner at the top.
2. **Hick's Law / Choice Overload.** Decision time grows with number of choices; prioritise and feature one. Apply: header has 6 icon buttons (Messages, Calendar, Notebook, Apps, theme, account) plus three cards each with lists and per-class menus. Reduce visible controls: move theme into the account menu, keep Messages/Calendar/Notebook/Apps as the pill (4 + account = 5 max). Per-class menu: show on hover/focus and always as one kebab, not several inline actions. Cap visible list items (see section 2).
3. **Cognitive Load / Working Memory / Miller's Law / Chunking.** Working memory holds about 4-7 chunks (page); extra elements are extraneous load. Apply: the page has roughly four chunks above the fold (hero, now tile, 3 counts, then cards). Make the above-fold region read as 3 chunks: greeting, "now" tile, a counts group. Group the three count tiles visually as one unit. Prefer recognition over recall: show "then X at time" (already there) so students do not remember the schedule; show which news is already read.
4. **Law of Common Region / Proximity / Uniform Connectedness / Similarity (Gestalt grouping).** Borders or backgrounds create groups; nearby items group; shared colour/shape implies shared function. Apply (flat, no shadows): use 1px border plus surface fill for card region, consistent 16px inner padding, 24px between cards, 8px between rows inside a card (inner gap smaller than outer gap). Count tiles share one style and a thin divider or one shared container so they read as a set. Same row anatomy across timetable, classes, news (icon/time, title, meta) so similar things look similar. Interactive text must differ from static text (colour plus underline or chevron).
5. **Fitts's Law.** Bigger, closer, well-spaced targets are faster. Apply: min 44x44 px touch targets on phone (page says "large enough", no number; 44 is the common guideline), 32-36 px minimum on desktop with 8 px gaps. Make whole rows and whole count tiles clickable, not just the label. Header pill icon buttons 40 px on desktop, 44 px on phone. Class kebab menu needs 44 px hit area even if the glyph is 20 px. Bottom sheets on phone already put targets in thumb reach (good); keep primary action at the bottom of the sheet.
6. **Occam's Razor / Tesler's Law / Pareto.** Remove elements that do not serve the goal; complexity moves to the system, not the user; about 20% of features give 80% of use. Apply: audit each element against "does a student need it daily". Likely daily: now-class, time left, today list, due soon, unread. Less often: Class updates tile, per-class menu, news pin/mark-read. Move the rare ones behind one tap (progressive disclosure) rather than permanent chrome. Let the system do work: auto-collapse past periods, auto-pick "today" (or next school day on weekends/after hours), auto-scroll timetable to the current period.
7. **Doherty Threshold.** Feedback within 400 ms; progress indicators and perceived performance help. Apply: render the glance strip and timetable from cache instantly (skeleton blocks, flat, no shimmer needed), then update. Tap feedback (pressed state) under 100 ms; popover/bottom sheet open under 400 ms. A day progress bar is already a progress indicator (good). Use skeletons for news photo so layout does not jump.
8. **Serial Position Effect.** First and last items are best remembered; put least important in the middle. Apply: order inside lists: put the most actionable first (current/next period; most recent unread). Header pill: put Messages first (most used) and account last; theme toggle goes in the middle or into the menu. Dashboard card order: Today, Classes, News (currently) is sensible; do not put News first.
9. **Goal-Gradient + Zeigarnik.** Progress toward a goal motivates; unfinished items are remembered. Apply: the day progress bar and "time left" already satisfy this, keep them. Due soon count is a Zeigarnik signal (open tasks), so show a count with the nearest due item's name and time ("Maths essay, tomorrow"). Ethical caution (Psychology of Design essay): no streaks, nagging, or manufactured urgency; keep badges honest and clearable.
10. **Jakob's Law / Mental Model / Familiar vs Novel.** People expect your site to work like others they know. Apply: schedule as a vertical time list, unread as a numeric badge, kebab for per-item menu, bell/envelope icons for messages. Novelty is allowed for brand (crest-blue tile) but not for interaction. Since the dashboard is changing for existing users, keep the same nouns (Due soon, Unread messages, Class updates, Today, Classes, News) and consider a one-time "what's new" dismissible note rather than a tour.
11. **Aesthetic-Usability Effect.** Pretty reads as more usable but can mask problems. Apply: flat style is fine; do not let polish replace testing. Test with 3-5 students on phone, check contrast in both themes (4.5:1 text, 3:1 UI) since looks can hide low-contrast problems.
12. **Law of Prägnanz.** People read the simplest form. Apply: prefer simple rectangles and one corner radius (e.g. 12 px cards, 8 px inner) throughout; no mixed shape languages. Day progress bar as plain rounded bar.
13. **Peak-End Rule.** Memory is dominated by peak and last moment. Apply: peak = the glance strip (class now, time left). End = bottom of the page and the empty/error states. Make "after school / weekend" state friendly (e.g. "School's out. Tomorrow starts with Science at 8:50"), and make errors (offline, session expired) calm, with a retry action. Finishing a day with nothing due is a positive peak: show "Nothing due soon" with a quiet check icon.
14. **Postel's Law.** Accept variable input, be conservative in output. Apply: handle odd data: no classes today, 0 unread, 200 unread (show 99+), missing news photo (text-only lead), long class names (truncate with title attribute), holidays/exams with irregular periods, timezone/period overlap.
15. **Paradox of the Active User.** Users start using without reading. Apply: no product tour; use short tooltips (title + aria-label) on icon-only header buttons, since the Cognitive Load essay warns icons alone are hard to interpret; add text labels where room allows (desktop tooltip on hover, phone bottom sheet titles).
16. **Flow.** Match challenge to skill, give feedback, remove friction. Apply: mark-read and pin give immediate visible change (optimistic update); no modal confirmations for reversible actions (offer Undo instead).
17. **Parkinson's Law.** Limit task time to what users expect. Apply: dashboard is a glance tool; aim for the answer to "what is on now / what is next / what is due" in under 3 seconds without scrolling.

**Already satisfied (keep):** day progress bar and time left (Goal-Gradient, Doherty); single crest-blue hero tile (Von Restorff); phone bottom sheets (Fitts); three equal cards and counts tiles (chunking, similarity) if styled consistently.

**Skipped as irrelevant:** Cognitive Bias (general background), Choice Overload (folded into Hick), Mental Model (folded into Jakob), the 2018 ethics note beyond the caution above, YouTube/Slack/Notion/Uber/Mailchimp case studies.

### 2. Top 5 concrete changes

1. **Make the hero glance strip the only strong-colour element and cut above-the-fold chunks to 3.** Keep the crest-blue now tile as the one filled brand block (about 2/3 width on desktop, full width on phone). Merge the three count tiles into one container (1px border, surface fill, thin 1px dividers between), neutral colour, with colour only on the number when count > 0. Count tiles use border/neutral by default; accent only when attention is needed (Von Restorff, Selective Attention, Common Region). Inside tile order: class name (largest), time left, "then X at time", progress bar.
2. **Cut visible choices (Hick/Occam/Serial Position).** Header pill max 5 controls: Messages, Calendar, Notebook, Apps, account. Move theme toggle into the account menu. Per-class: one kebab only, shown at all times on phone but fade-in on hover/focus on desktop (still keyboard reachable). Cap lists: Today shows all periods but collapses finished ones into "Earlier (N)"; Classes show up to 8 then "All classes"; News shows 1 lead plus 4 items then "More news". Pin/mark-read inside a menu or on hover, not permanent.
3. **Fix target sizes and spacing (Fitts, Proximity).** Phone: all tappable rows min height 48 px, icon buttons 44x44, gap between adjacent targets 8 px minimum. Desktop: rows min 40 px, icon buttons 36-40 px. Whole row and whole count tile are the click target. Spacing scale: 8 px between rows, 16 px card padding, 24 px between cards, 32 px between hero and cards (outer gap always larger than inner gap).
4. **Make the Due soon tile informative and state-aware (Working Memory, Zeigarnik, Peak-End, Postel).** Show the count plus the nearest item ("Maths essay, tomorrow") so recognition replaces recall. Cap badge display at 99+. Provide calm empty states: "Nothing due soon", "No unread messages", "School's out, next: X at time". After school hours, the hero tile shows the next school day's first class, not blank.
5. **Performance and feedback budget (Doherty, Flow).** Paint from cached data in under 100 ms using flat skeleton blocks sized to final layout (no layout shift, reserve news photo ratio e.g. 16:9); press feedback under 100 ms; popovers and sheets open under 200 ms (<400 ms hard limit); optimistic updates for mark-read/pin with a 5 s Undo toast instead of confirm dialogs; update live countdown at most once per minute to avoid change blindness and needless repaints; announce changes with aria-live=polite only for count changes.

Honourable mention: consistent row anatomy across all three cards (leading icon or time, title, meta, trailing action), same radius (12 px cards, 8 px inner), icon buttons always carry a tooltip and aria-label.
